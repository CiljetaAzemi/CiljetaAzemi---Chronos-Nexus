# Civilization objective audit and evaluation

## Banded security-priority policy

The opt-in `--balanced-priority[=BASIS_POINTS]` policy compares fixed bands of four losses, then uses the existing expanded score to break ties. Set `"objective_model": "balanced-priority"` in scenario JSON for the default 100-basis-point (1%) width. The historical policy definitions and requirement-coverage matrix below remain applicable.

Let `w = basis_points / 10,000`. For a nonnegative loss `L` with fixed reference `D`, use band `floor((L / max(1,D)) / w + 1e-9)`. The losses, in order, are one minus population-weighted essential coverage (zero when the component is unavailable), unmet colony Food and Medicine consumption, ending colony Food and Medicine shortage, and one minus timely external fulfillment. Their references are 1, initial modeled critical demand over the horizon, initial critical stock targets, and 1. These are fixed from the scenario. The policy lexicographically minimizes the four bands and then maximizes expanded score rounded to 1e-9. It uses common declared obligations during urgent displacement. A width bounds differences inside a band. Even a tiny change can cross a boundary, so it provides no blanket indifference guarantee.

This ordering is transitive because each plan has a fixed tuple. Earlier incumbents require no rescoring when a new candidate appears. Every replacement strictly improves the tuple and has already passed existing feasibility checks. Equal keys retain the first validated candidate. Unavoidable shortages simply populate the corresponding bands, so they create no additional feasibility condition. Comparing already evaluated metrics costs constant time and space. No growing archive is kept. The existing search and replay costs still dominate end-to-end complexity.

Keep legacy as the default. The banded policy preserved the critical-security gains and saved some operating cost in the indexed scarcity case, while retaining only a small share of the general unmet-consumption improvement. It found no cheaper equivalent in the urgent-event candidate pool and regressed on held-out production cut and Large unmet consumption relative to strict priority. Extreme measured recovery of a validated partial plan under a deadline, with under 1% external mission assignment. [Experimental results](benchmark-results/README.md)

The study compares 25, 100 and 250 basis points on the development scarcity case. These widths are experimental sensitivity settings, with no claim of statistical calibration or official status. The independent candidate-pool audit compares the two weighted policies, strict and banded priorities, a feasible-reference constraint, and raw-outcome Pareto dominance. Its constraint holds strict security coordinates and a service floor equal to the lower of the legacy and strict selections, then minimizes fuel, transport, waste and risk in that order. The strict reference is itself admissible. Pareto labels use only the observed common candidate pool and do not imply global efficiency. Reference constraints and Pareto archives were kept offline because reference discovery can occur late, and a changing reference can invalidate the interpretation of an earlier incumbent.

The banded selector reuses the same nominal replay, indexed aid, and urgent-displacement comparison points as strict priority. Ordinary allocation, scheduling and spacecraft scoring still propose candidates. Route planning and proactive delivery generation remain unchanged. The common-pool audit found a less fuel-intensive urgent-event schedule with lower coverage and timely fulfillment, and no schedule that met the strict reference and saved operating cost. The indexed scarcity comparison changed the infrastructure selection and aid augmentation together, so the aggregate savings cannot be attributed to a particular supplier, spacecraft, or route. This evidence supports testing the existing proposals under a different ordering. It does not isolate a particular replacement, consolidation, or cargo-size rule that would improve the result.

With `C` feasible candidate projections and `D` recorded coordinates, the offline Pareto audit uses `O(C²D)` worst-case work and `O(CD)` storage. It can stop a dominance scan at the first witness. The offline reference experiment sorts at most `C` rows in `O(C log C)` time. Neither cost is on the runtime selection path. The controlled exact banded fixture checks all four outcomes and every comparison triple, including deterministic ties and a cheaper plan within the same coverage band. The best fixture plan earns a strictly better coverage band despite lower service and a lower expanded score. No global bound on the heuristic gap follows.

The original specification's Civilization Objective section distinguishes civilization outcomes from the possible final competition formula. This implementation includes an opt-in expanded weighted score and retains the previous score for direct comparisons. The model and coefficients are experimental.

## Previous objective

Write `p(x,r) = x/(x + max(1,r))`. Let `E` be total ending shortage, `U` unserved consumption, `T` the sum of stock targets, and `D` the sum of consumption rates multiplied by the scenario horizon. A subscript `c` restricts sums to food and medicine at colony locations. Let `C` be external cargo requested, `N` the number of external missions, `B` the construction budget, and `I` aggregate positive strategic value of all listed projects.

| Term | Exact legacy definition |
|---|---|
| Resource security | `1 - (p(E,T) + p(U,D))/2` |
| Critical security | `1 - (p(Ec,Tc) + p(Uc,Dc))/2`, or the general security term if there are no colonies |
| Service | Timely accepted cargo clipped to each requested amount and weighted by `max(0,priority)`, divided by the sum of identically weighted requests. Zero if the denominator is zero. |
| Infrastructure | Selected strategic value divided by `I`, or zero when `I = 0`. |
| Operating penalty | Mean of `p(transport credits,C)`, `p(fuel units,C)`, and `p(construction credits,B)`. |
| Waste penalty | `p(waste,C+D)`. |
| Risk penalty | `p(additive route risk,N)`. |

The formula is the weighted sum of the first four terms minus the weighted sum of the last three. Balanced weights are `30,25,20,5,10,5,5`, security weights are `35,35,10,5,5,5,5`, and service weights are `20,15,40,5,10,5,5`. Custom nonnegative weights are normalized to sum to 100. Given valid finite outcomes and normal construction accounting, the loose balanced bound is `[-20,80]` (security `[-15,85]`, service `[-20,80]`). Finite feasible outcomes need not reach these endpoints.

A failed or omitted mission gets zero timely credit. A delayed delivery earns credit only if it still arrives by its deadline. A timely partial delivery earns proportional credit. The legacy formula treats priority zero as zero-weight service. The nominal objective includes initial external obligations, while realized scoring also includes external urgent requests activated through the horizon, whether served or unserved. Generated aid and preventive trips affect stock and operating outcomes. Their cargo does not enter either model's external service reference. Candidate replay uses known production effects. Realized simulation adds the actual event stream. JSON's exported nominal forecast also includes its preventive supply pass, so it can differ from the nominal score used to select the earlier candidate.

## Coverage against the specification

| Objective | Legacy coverage | Expanded coverage and evidence still needed |
|---|---|---|
| Population survival | Indirect through critical colony stock | Population-weighted modeled coverage of essential consumption. Actual survival requires demographic or health data and a validated consequence model. |
| Resource security | Direct ending shortage and unserved consumption, with critical amounts also included in general security | Normalized per-account ending and unserved fractions. Account priority supplies relative importance. Population-covered consumption enters coverage only. |
| Economic growth | Missing as a separate outcome. Production affects stock. | Optional incremental economic development appraisal. Stock effects still come from the simulator. Real growth needs output quantities, valuations, and an economic baseline. |
| Scientific progress | Missing. Location technology is a static input. | Optional research appraisal assigned to an eligible completed project. No technology progression rate is inferred. Observed progress requires milestones or simulated technology changes. |
| Territorial expansion | Missing. Existing colony locations are inputs. | Optional incremental colony-development appraisal. The project must be selected and finish within the horizon. No discoveries, new territory, or acquired area are inferred. |
| Mission success | Direct timely priority-weighted fulfillment | Direct timely fulfillment with positive minimum weight for each external obligation. |
| Transportation cost | Measured route distance times ship operating cost, combined with other penalties | Explicit normalized transportation penalty using the same physical accounting. |
| Resource waste | Direct simulated waste | Explicit normalized waste using fixed reference cargo and consumption. |
| Mission failures | Indirect through lost timely fulfillment | Explicit external service loss `1 - service`, counted once. Raw failure counts remain separate. |
| Risk | Direct additive route-risk exposure | Explicit normalized exposure. Reliability and uncertainty also affect feasibility and route choice. This exposure is not a probability. |
| Energy consumption | Fuel proxy inside operating penalty | Separate fuel penalty. An energy measure requires fuel conversion or direct per-route energy data. |
| Computational cost | External benchmark timing and memory | Still external. Including runtime in decisions would change the meaning of otherwise identical plans and could double-count competition penalties. |

## Expanded mathematical formulation

Each utility is on `[0,1]`. At resource account `a`, divide ending shortage by its positive stock target, and unmet consumption by its positive horizon demand. Clip each loss to one. General security is one minus the weighted mean of the applicable account losses with weight `1 + log(1 + max(priority,0))`. A target of zero contributes no ending term. Zero consumption contributes no consumption term. Critical consumption with population coverage is taken out of this mean so it is counted only in the coverage term.

For each populated location with modeled food or medicine consumption, divide its population equally across the applicable essential accounts. Coverage is one minus the mean unmet essential fraction using these population weights. The proxy measures whether essential consumption is served at populated locations. It estimates neither lives saved nor mortality. Locations without positive essential consumption do not inflate coverage.

The three development utilities divide the sum of selected, eligible project appraisals by the sum of the corresponding appraisal over the fixed eligible project catalog. Eligibility requires a proposed project affordable individually and scheduled to complete within the horizon. JSON accepts nonnegative `economic_appraisal`, `research_appraisal`, and `colony_appraisal`, defaulting to zero. These are scenario-provided estimates of incremental benefits distinct from inventory effects. A scenario author must avoid duplicating the same benefit in multiple appraisals. Appraisals use no assumed growth rate. Selected project IDs are checked against the eligible catalog and combined budget. Without a declared appraisal the component is unavailable.

Service uses the legacy timely cargo ratio with importance `max(1,priority)` per unit. Mission loss is the complementary fraction. Actual cost, fuel, risk, waste and construction spending use the same rational loss function `p` with references `C, C, N, C+D, B`, respectively. Fuel stands for the modeled fuel burden, with no conversion to electricity. The utility for each cost is one minus the loss. Waste and construction spending split one weight equally. An unnecessary trip cannot increase service on its own, and an unused construction project with no declared incremental benefit earns no development utility. A trip may improve both obligation fulfillment and resource security because contract service and stock availability are separate outcomes.

| Utility | Balanced | Security | Service |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Essential coverage proxy | 20 | 25 | 15 |
| Resource security | 25 | 35 | 15 |
| External timely fulfillment | 25 | 10 | 40 |
| Economic appraisal | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| Research appraisal | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| Colony appraisal | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| Transportation | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| Fuel | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| Waste and construction spending | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| Route risk | 4 | 4 | 4 |

The balanced profile emphasizes measurable security and service. Coverage receives less than either to reflect proxy uncertainty. Each declared development appraisal receives a small equal weight because the simulator has no observed development trajectory. Transportation and fuel have equal weights for distinct monetary and physical burdens. Waste and budget share a smaller weight, and risk receives a separate weight. The security and service profiles move weight among the three main benefits and keep the cost preferences fixed. These are policy choices for sensitivity testing, with no statistical calibration.

Expanded score is `100 * sum(w_i * utility_i) / sum(active w_i)`, bounded by zero and 100. Coverage, security, service and appraisals only participate when their fixed references are positive. Cost and risk utilities always participate, with reference floors of one. Unavailable benefits do not earn free points. Scaling requested cargo and project appraisals before summing preserves their ratios while avoiding overflow in large finite inputs. Identical algorithms are compared on the same input, active components and weights. Cross-scenario scores can mix different active components and reference scales. Compare raw outcomes alongside paired scores.

## Integration and performance

Phase 3 compares the existing feasible replay candidates under the selected objective. Expanded mode proposes an additional appraised construction portfolio if its selection differs, and uses appraisals instead of generic strategic value in the deficit-targeted construction candidate. Indexed feasibility-aware allocation accepts an augmented schedule only after feasibility replay and improvement under the same objective. Urgent displacement uses the same model for keep/replace, with obligations known at event time and common projected contributions. Baseline allocation, per-mission ordering, spacecraft and route searches, and the preventive planner remain heuristic proposal mechanisms. The objective selects among tested plans. It does not solve the complete planning problem optimally.

With `A` accounts, `M` external obligations, `P` project inputs, and `K` selected projects, expanded setup takes expected `O(A+M+P)` time and space. An evaluation with an empty scenario takes constant time. In general it takes expected `O(A+M+K)` time, with `O(K)` extra space to detect duplicate selections. This assumes ordinary hash-table behavior. Pathological collisions can make hash lookup linear in container size. The bounded candidate set may add one Knapsack call and one replay when appraisals produce a distinct portfolio. The extra Knapsack call costs `O(PB)` time for integral budget `B`, with `O(B+PB)` worst-case storage for the reconstruction records. No all-candidate replay was added to individual routing or assignment checks.

## Competition boundary and reproduction

The professor's possible competition formula combines quality, time, memory and risk or operating cost. The coefficients and exact quality definition are unknown. The internal expanded score already accounts for risk and operating costs. A competition evaluator should use raw outcomes or separate benefit and cost terms so those costs are charged only once. The project exports raw cost, fuel, waste, shortage and risk alongside wall time and peak working set. There is no claimed official competition score.

Set `"objective_model": "expanded"` and optionally `"objective_profile": "security"` or `"service"` in scenario JSON. JSON returns both scores for the same plan. For benchmarks use `--expanded-objective` with Phase 3. [Experimental reproduction](benchmark-results/README.md)

For the scaling and preference interpretation of weighted sums, see Marler and Arora, *The weighted sum method for multi-objective optimization: new insights*. [Primary research article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00158-009-0460-7)

## Security-priority decision experiment

The original specification requires attempting to improve survival, security, development and service while limiting operational and computational burdens. It leaves ordering and trade-offs to the team. The coverage matrix above still applies. The new policy changes comparison, and introduces no new demographic, scientific, economic, territorial or energy model. The two historical score formulas remain available for cross-scoring. No competition coefficients are assumed.

### Reproduced failure mechanisms

For the six previous balanced Small and Medium comparisons, the expanded denominator was 88 because the three four-point development appraisals were unavailable. Forgoing construction alone added 0.80–1.14 expanded points against legacy plans. This exceeded the entire net score gain in five cases. Production improvements still counted through stock and consumption, but the weighted score could prefer saving construction expenditure despite worse ending stock. Normalizing losses by account targets and weighting accounts by importance also means that a lower unweighted total shortage is not guaranteed.

In Medium scarcity, the indexed aid result and ordinary expanded result had identical external service. The indexed result had lower transport and fuel penalties, by 0.05157 and 0.05358, and lower risk penalty, by 0.01273. Before the 100/88 score scaling, those three savings added about 0.577 weighted points. General security and essential coverage together lost about 0.062 weighted points. Critical ending shortages and critical unmet consumption rose from 170 and 3 to 350 and 30. The expanded formula gives no separate weight to the legacy critical-security metric. General security averages normalized ending losses, and coverage measures population-weighted unmet consumption over populated locations. A colony's safety-stock deficit and its actual unserved consumption are different outcomes.

The Medium urgent-event replay reproduced 208 completions, 200 critical ending shortage and 1,213 unserved consumption under anytime expanded selection, against 255, 150 and 160.321 for unbudgeted expanded planning. It also reduced fuel and transport. The anytime path adds feasible external-only prefixes and the empty alternative. The weighted selector can prefer a plan that the unbudgeted generator never presents. Small scarcity exhibited this directly: unbudgeted expanded planning served six missions while the anytime weighted comparison retained the empty incumbent. No denominator depended on planner acceptance. These are preference and candidate-set effects. The comparisons do not establish that a better joint allocation, route or mission chain is unavailable.

### Compared decision rules

| Rule | Definition on a fixed feasible candidate set | Consequence observed or established |
|---|---|---|
| Weighted sum | Maximize the legacy or expanded formula above | Supports compensation. The reproduced cost and stock trade-offs show that small normalized coverage or stock changes can lose to operational savings. |
| Strict lexicographic comparison | Compare essential coverage, then lower colony essential unserved, then lower colony essential ending shortage, then higher timely external service, then expanded score | Provides a fixed order across prefixes. A small improvement at a higher rank can justify a large cost. |
| Reference-relative epsilon constraint | Among plans with coverage at least `C_ref − εC`, critical shortages at most `S_ref + εS`, and service at least `F_ref − εF`, maximize the expanded score | A feasible reference always satisfies these lower and upper bounds, including unavoidable shortages. For the Medium scarcity ordinary-versus-indexed pair, zero ending tolerance excludes the indexed outcome; an allowance of 180 ending units and roughly 0.000072 coverage fraction admits it if the service threshold is unchanged. These are observed switching points, not policy targets. A reference obtained late during planning would require reconsidering earlier incumbents. |
| Pareto dominance | Compare componentwise on retained benefits and burdens, with at least one strict improvement for dominance | The Medium scarcity plans are mutually nondominated because security and cost improve in opposite directions. Dominance removes a replicated outcome with strictly more fuel, but it cannot select a winner for the scarcity trade-off. |

Only strict lexicographic comparison is implemented as a new selector. Use `--security-priority` for Phase 3, or `"objective_model": "security-priority"` in scenario JSON. Its fixed key maximizes `(coverage, −critical unserved, −critical ending shortage, timely service, expanded score)` in that order. Coordinates are rounded to a fixed 1e-9 grid for reproducible numerical ties, with very large magnitudes kept as represented. An unavailable coverage component compares equally across the scenario. Colony critical quantities are Food and Medicine totals in modeled resource units, with the known limitation that these units need not be interchangeable. Coverage, as a flow measure, has precedence over end-of-horizon stock. Service ranks ahead of operational savings on security ties. The expanded profile changes only the final score tie-break. There is no minimum absolute stock threshold, and an empty plan can still win when available assignments worsen a higher-ranked component. Strict priority can also favor costly service or small stock gains. This makes the policy experimental.

Higher-ranked coordinates impose ordering without adding another numerical security reward to the expanded score. Full and timely partial fulfillment are credited against the same original demand. Generated aid earns no external service credit, so an otherwise unnecessary shipment with the same security and service loses on the cost tie-break if it adds a measured burden. Optional appraisals participate only when the fixed eligible catalog supplies a reference. This still depends on scenario authors declaring distinct, substantively justified benefits. Fuel and transport remain separate model quantities. If a future official evaluator charges for fuel within monetary transport or charges risk and operating costs separately, its quality term should use raw benefits or remove the overlapping penalties.

The valid-comparison set still relies on existing route, capacity, fuel, deadline, inventory and construction feasibility. Phase 3 tries the same mission-ordering, spacecraft, route and construction generation rules, plus the feasible no-action option in this mode. Indexed aid uses the comparator after replay. Urgent displacement uses the same comparator on common projected contributions and currently activated obligations. The spare-capacity urgent insertion and proactive shortage heuristics still propose and schedule deliveries using their existing checks. The security-priority comparator neither proposes new routes nor optimizes every individual trade. A donor shipment that harms a higher-ranked resource outcome can still be generated and later rejected. Declared mission importance continues to affect generation; the previously published expanded service formula still weights fulfilled cargo by priority.

Comparing five already evaluated coordinates takes constant time and space in best and worst cases. No average-case input distribution is assumed. Evaluation and replay keep their prior bounds, with one additional no-action replay in Phase 3 candidate selection. No Pareto archive is retained. Controlled regressions enumerate four projected outcomes, including a fuel-dominated outcome, and verify coverage priority under an expensive emergency, service on security ties, and acceptance of an incumbent with unavoidable shortages. On coverage, service, transport and fuel, the nondominated projections are the cheap half-service plan, cheap full-service plan with lower coverage, and higher-coverage emergency plan. The emergency variant with extra fuel is dominated. A separate single-spacecraft fixture enumerates the empty schedule, its only feasible first mission, and the feasible two-mission chain. The anytime planner reaches the maximum key with full service. The alternate slower first route is dominated on fuel for the single-mission sequence and misses the second mission's deadline for the chain. These are ordering results on tiny fixtures. No scalar optimality gap is defined for the lexicographic key, and no global guarantee follows.
